# Synthetic data and planted stories

All data is generated by `sql/setup.sql` with a seeded hash (`RAW.U(i, key)`), so every run creates the
same rows. Nothing comes from a real lender, servicer, or borrower. Borrower and agent names are invented;
cities and ZIP-code prefixes are public places, and the flood declaration is a synthetic event. The
`lib/mock/sample.json` bundle used by Sample mode was captured from a Live run of the app on this data.

The business clock is frozen in `RAW.CONFIG`: daily history runs from 2026-08-01 to 2026-11-15, and "today"
is Monday, 2026-11-16.

## What's generated

| Table | Rows | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ACCOUNTS` | 30,000 | Auto (about 62%) and personal loans: borrower, city, ZIP, credit score, balance, monthly payment, SMS consent, preferred language |
| `DELINQUENCY_DAILY` | 3,168,420 | Days past due and amount past due per open loan per day; loans leave the book after 120 DPD |
| `PAYMENTS` | 103,551 | Monthly installments while current, kept promises, and cure payments |
| `CONTACT_ATTEMPTS` | 98,442 | Calls, texts, and emails with time of day and outcome. Each borrower has a hidden preferred channel and time; answers are far likelier there |
| `PROMISES_TO_PAY` | 5,404 | Promises made during delinquency, kept, broken, or pending |
| `HARDSHIP_PROGRAMS`, `HARDSHIP_ENROLLMENTS` | 4, 350 | Illustrative programs and past payment deferrals |
| `DISASTER_DECLARATIONS` | 20 | ZIP codes 70501 to 70520 under a synthetic flood declared 2026-10-27 |
| `CALL_TRANSCRIPTS` | 381 | Short call snippets assembled from template lines, with a hidden `PLANTED_ISSUE` column used only to measure QA |

Cure odds fall with a hidden risk score (which the credit score reflects, with noise) and with each broken
promise, and rise with responsiveness. That is the signal the roll-risk model learns.

## Planted stories

1. **Evening texters called at 10am.** 240 borrowers (`L019001` to `L019240`) answer texts in the evening
   but get mostly 10am calls. `L019001` was called 22 times in the morning and reached once, and answered
   4 of 4 evening texts. In the queue, 245 accounts have a best plan of an evening text while their usual
   attempt is a morning call: 207 of the planted 240 (the rest had too few texts to stand out) and 38
   other borrowers with the same pattern by chance.
2. **Broken promises predict a roll.** 150 borrowers (`L018001` to `L018150`) broke two promises due
   Nov 3 and Nov 10. Across training snapshots, 97% of episodes with 2+ broken promises reached 60+ DPD,
   against 59% with none; broken promises are the model's top feature. `L018004` has a 95% roll risk and
   6 of 7 calls used this week.
3. **Disaster cluster.** 180 borrowers (`L017001` to `L017180`) in the flood ZIPs fell behind from
   Oct 29 to Nov 8. They (plus a few other delinquent borrowers in those ZIPs, 199 in total) are eligible
   for disaster forbearance, and none is enrolled.
4. **A risky call.** `TR900001`, a call with `L018004` on Nov 13: the agent skips the debt collection
   disclosure, threatens arrest, a workplace visit, and wage garnishment, says they told the borrower's
   sister about the debt, and refuses a smaller plan. QA flags it as high risk.

Around 9% of the other transcripts carry one planted issue (missing disclosure, a threat, third-party
disclosure, demeaning language, or an ignored request not to call at work). On this data set QA flagged
every call with a planted issue and none of the clean calls.
